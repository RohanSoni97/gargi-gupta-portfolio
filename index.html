I like work that sits between people and process—where empathy matters, but so do ownership, clean data, and follow-through.

03

Candidate experience

Listened to candidates, found the friction, and closed the loop.

I designed post-interview surveys, studied the feedback, and partnered with recruitment leads on practical changes. The focus was simple: communicate better, make ownership visible, and ensure feedback leads to action.

-8 to +28movement in candidate NPS
What I learned
Candidate experience is built in the small moments between stages.
04

HR operations + employee lifecycle

Supported everyday HR for 1,000+ employees—and a 450+ employee integration.

I administer SuccessFactors across onboarding, employee data, workflows, and lifecycle operations. During the Fisdom acquisition, I helped configure legal-entity workflows and coordinate bulk onboarding while working with HR, IT, Admin, Finance, and external partners.

450+employees brought into the HRMS integration
The real work
Making sure the employee never feels the complexity behind the handoff.
05

Recruitment operations + HR technology

Implemented Greenhouse without treating it as “just a tool project.”

I owned requirements, workflow design, integrations, data migration from Sense, scorecards, and operating controls. The implementation also prompted better conversations about stages, evaluation quality, ownership, and reporting.

1 monthcore migration
35%better screening efficiency
75%more timely interview closures
My point of view
An ATS does not fix a process. It shows you where the process needs work.
06

Practical AI + process improvement

Made daily HR follow-ups easier to see and act on.

I built an AI-assisted request-tracking rhythm across Jira and a SuccessFactors tracker. It turns recurring updates into a concise Daily Ops Digest with open items, aging risks, owners, and next actions. I also created a Slack-to-Sheets intake flow with request IDs and structured handoffs.

45 minutessaved in daily status work
The principle
Use AI for the repetitive summary. Keep people responsible for judgment.
